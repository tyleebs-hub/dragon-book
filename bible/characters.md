## Bram (protagonist)
- Village boy from Kell's Ford, 16 at recruitment. Physically strong, trained by fighting and sparring with the other boys under Maddoc.
- Mother died of fever when he was nine. Lives with his father Tobin, the tanner, at the downwind end of the village. Tobin is who he leaves behind.
- Has a nascent spark of magic. That spark is why he must go with the wizard even though he resists.
- **Inner life:** struggles to know what is right and what is true. Overthinks, doubts himself, gets stuck. Outwardly he can look decisive and dangerous (especially through Hob's eyes); inwardly he second-guesses constantly.
- The wizard wants to teach him that physical prowess and will are not enough. The story partly proves the wizard right and partly wrong.
- Kills Hob in a bout that goes too long (see Hob). Kills again defending the carriage from bandits. Both mark him.
- Does poorly in his studies at the school.
- Begins to sympathize with the dragon during the school's hunt.
- After the bond: speaks telepathically with Vharis only. Other dragons he feels loosely, as energy or pressure, never in words. Draws on Vharis's power.
- Rides his own horse or walks most of the time, even after he can fly.
- Later: builds and holds a fortress; attracts human followers.

**Decided**
- Age 16. Leaves his father Tobin behind.
- The group's phrase is "Never stay down." Hob hears it as endurance: you always get back up. Bram hears it as a rule about the other man: the fight is not over while he can still get up.

**Open questions**
- How does he leave the school: default is that he simply vanishes after the bond and the school presumes him dead. When does the school learn otherwise?

## Hob (opening POV)
- A boy from Bram's village and training group, 15. We meet Bram only through Hob's eyes at first.
- Hob lives by the group's phrase, "Never stay down," and hears it as endurance. When the POV shifts to Bram, Bram reads the same phrase differently.
- Hob wants badly to be chosen by the wizard. Bram does not.
- Hob dies at Bram's hand in chapter 3, from his own POV.

**Decided**
- The death: Orvane asks to see the boys fight. Hob draws Bram. The bout goes long. Bram could stop and does not, and at the end his spark flares. Neither a clean accident nor a clean choice; Bram can never be sure how much was him. Orvane's eyes are on Bram the whole time.
- Orvane's offer afterward is also the village's way to be rid of Bram.
- Hob carries chapters 1 to 3.

## Master Orvane (the wizard, mentor)
- Serves a large human kingdom. Fiercely loyal to it, like all wizards.
- Political, egotistical, ambitious, and often successful. The vibe: a smug smile you want to punch off his face. He is usually right enough to be unbearable.
- Appearance (Ch 2): about fifty, middling height, soft and well-kept, good black coat, rings, close grey beard, thinning hair, round clever face. Voice carries without being raised. Says "correct" the way other men say "small."
- His magic costs him. After putting out and relighting a hearth fire his right hand trembles; he hides it. Keep this in every scene where he works.
- Claims to have met four dragons. Was at the Narrows "after" and recognized Maddoc's regiment on sight.
- Recruits gifted youths to prepare for the coming dragon apocalypse.
- Arrives at Kell's Ford looking for talent. Asks to see the boys fight; this is the bout in which Hob dies. Watches Bram throughout.
- Stages a fake bandit ambush to humble Bram. It goes wrong (someone paid the bandits more; who is open).
- During the ambush he is in a deep magical slumber, communing with fellow wizards and boasting about the boy he personally found.
- Later confronts Bram at his fortress and criticizes him. Bram counters that he has done more to prepare than anyone.

**Open questions**
- Is Orvane the one wizard who wants to unite magic users across kingdoms, or is that someone else? (See world.md.)
- Does he ever admit the ambush was staged? When, and to whom?

## Vharis (the dragon)
- An old dragon. In spirit, an old man content with the humble life he has lived. He doesn't yearn for more, especially if reaching for it risks what he has.
- Lairs in a cave unusually close to the school.
- Lays waste to much of the school's expedition, but is wounded.
- Bonded to Bram by accident when his blood drops into Bram's wound. Tried, and failed, to kill him.
- Woke first after the bonding and carried Bram to a new, more distant cave before more wizards could come.
- Male. Refuses to let Bram ride him. Bram can find his own way or grow his own wings.
- **Why he helps (decided):** the bond makes Bram's danger his danger. When Bram is hurt, Vharis feels it. If Bram dies, Vharis is not sure he survives it. So he helps to keep the boy alive, complaining the whole way, and never admits he has come to care. This is also why he did not kill Bram after waking.
- Later injured or captured during Bram's attack on a human castle.

**Decided (author, 2026-11-06):** Bram drives the expansion. Vharis helps, but grudgingly: he never wants it, says so, and does not pretend otherwise. **Still open:** what keeps him helping instead of refusing or leaving (see open questions below).

**Open questions**
- Color, size?
- (Dropped 2026-11-06: the "second adolescence" suggestion. Vharis has no ambition of his own; Bram supplies all of it.)

## Queen Ysolde (POV)
- Incredibly smart, very detail oriented.
- Mostly frustrated with people who don't believe in her and doubt her capabilities no matter what she does. Not framed as a sexism story; the doubt has other roots.

**Decided**
- Rules a kingdom neighboring Caldreth (Orvane's). Name TBD.
- She is one of the few rulers who believes the apocalypse is real, and is dismissed for exactly that.
- Her arc: deciding whether to treat with a boy and a dragon, or with the wizards who sneer at her.
- She is not the ruler whose castle Bram attacks (that is the bonding king).

**Open questions**
- Other roots of the doubt (young? irregular succession?).

## The bonding king (working name King Aldric)
- Obsessed with getting his soldiers bonded to dragons. Exhausts his kingdom's resources and men capturing dragons and wounding men to force the bond. It keeps failing.
- At the end of his storyline, his men turn on him, cut off his arms, and throw him to a captured dragon to be eaten. Instead, he and the dragon bond.

**Decided**
- Bram attacks this king's castle because he can feel the caged dragons there. Vharis is taken during that attack.
- His bond works because both he and the dragon are at the edge of death (see magic.md). His experiments always kept the men alive and the dragons healthy.
- His chapters are told from the POV of an officer in his service who is among the men who turn on him.

**Open questions**
- Does he survive? (Dragon eats his men and they escape?)
- Both arms, or one?

## Kell's Ford (established in Ch 1)
- **Maddoc:** retired sergeant who runs the drill ground. Three fingers on his left hand, stiff right leg. Served 18 to 30 in the Third Foot under Harrow, was at the Narrows; home 20 years, paid in flour and firewood. Never talks about the wars. Has known about Bram's spark for three years ("since the pot") and has seen once before what an untrained spark does. Stopped sparring Bram two summers ago; told Bram three summers ago never to strike the head. Has noticed the spark and says nothing.
- **Tobin:** Bram's father, the tanner, downwind end of the village. Wife died of fever when Bram was nine; has barely spoken since.
- **Hob's family:** father the ferryman; mother (sat with Bram's dying mother, sends bread to the tannery); blind grandmother "Nan"; Jory, eldest, inherits the ferry; Nell, 5; two more small ones. Names for the parents still open.
- **Other boys:** Dace (loud), Wat and Pim (arguing brothers; Pim is kind to Hob), Ansel (small, quick), three unnamed.
- **Marran:** keeps the alehouse. **Jorrey:** the smith. **Hallam:** the reeve, eleven years, can read. **Old Garan:** his father once saw a dragon asleep on a rock past Ferrin. Dace's surname is Miller.
- **Fenn:** Orvane's outrider, lean, grey coat, heron badge, dry. Spoke to Hob at the ferry and nodded to him in the crowd.
- **Orvane's two soldiers (Ch 2):** an older one with grey in his beard, watchful; a young one barely older than Jory who fidgets with his sword and grins at girls. Unnamed. They die in the ambush.
